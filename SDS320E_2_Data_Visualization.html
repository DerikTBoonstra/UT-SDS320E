Consideration while Visualizing Data: Overplotting

  • Visualizing your data can be fun! we will learn all type of ways to make graphs and customization options
  • However, over-plotting is very common and can obscure the intention of the graph
  • Edward Tufte coined the terms:
    • Data ink: ink used in a visualization necessary to convey meaning
    • Data-ink ratio: ratio of data-ink to non-data ink

Tip

Consideration while Visualizing Data: Color Usage

  • Unless you are color blind, most do not consider how others perceive color in graphs
    • Around 1% of females are color blind, but around 10% of males are at least partially color blind source:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_blindness
  • Color consideration goes past color blindess:
    • Using similar colors for different categories can infer they are actually the same
    • Using green and red may infer positive/negative or good/bad
    • While becoming less popular, people still print graphics, and often in black and white

Consideration while Visualizing Data: Color Usage Cont.

  • Rainbow colors are not color blind friendly

Plotting in R

  1. Base R plot() is the native plotting function in R and does not require packages

  2. ggplot() is from the ggplot2 package and allows for layered and customizable plots

ggplot: Grammer of Graphics

In brief, the grammar [of graphics] tells us that a statistical graphic is a mapping from data to aesthetic attributes (colour, shape, size) of geometric objects (points, lines, bars). - Hadley Wickham

  • Aesthetic: visual property of an object Think: color, size, transparency, etc.
  • Aesthetic mapping: a relation between variables and visual elements \(aes:variables \longrightarrow aesthetics\)

Plotting in R: ggplot()
